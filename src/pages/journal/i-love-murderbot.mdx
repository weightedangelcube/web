Recently it suddenly occurred to me that books were things that existed, and
that I did love reading, and wouldn't it be great to start doing that instead of
scrolling social media? I remember finding Martha Wells's book _All Systems Red_
in a queer books list, thinking it was interesting enough, and borrowing it from
the public library.

Oh, boy. Oh gosh.

_**Spoilers for the series, if you haven't caught on to that yet. Go read it.
Seriously, it's awesome.**_

Nothing could prepare me for how much I was about to _adore_ this story about a
security android trying to figure out its own identity. I never really
considered myself a sci-fi person before _(this sounds stupid in hindsight,
Portal 2 is my favourite video game)_ but oh boy, was I one now.

I always find it hard to describe the things I like, and why I like them.
Perhaps it's the reluctant yet effortless way Murderbot does its job. _(It's
really badass.)_ Perhaps it's the scorn and disdain it has for humans, and
perhaps it's how it still gives its all to protect them.

Perhaps it's because Murderbot is on a lonely quest to discover who it is, and
perhaps so am I.

1. All Systems Red

I love Murderbot. _(I said that already.)_ Murderbot is shy and awkward and
incredibly compelling, and I loved to see it grow from just doing its job to
perhaps taking its place amongst the humans. There's not even that much I can
say about the first book outside of it being just so great. It's awesome. Go
read it.

2. Artificial Condition

Murderbot gets confronted, ooh... I love ART and I love its ability to get
Murderbot to admit it doesn't know what the fuck it's doing. I'm sure ART
relishes it, too—_(I know I would)_—they make such a great duo.

It's also here that we get to see for ourselves just how much Murderbot cares
about the wellbeing of its clients—clients that it just met. _(It's kinda cute,
really.)_ Murderbot expects the mining site to reveal its true nature, and
leaves feeling so empty... knowing that the answer is still to be sought within
itself. And it kind of does find that answer within the complexities of it all.
That's why it returns to help Mensah, isn't it? This is such a heartwarming book
despite all the action/adventure business.

Also, ART shortened Murderbot's arms and legs! There's honestly probably some
trans allegory to be found here, but I'll leave that up to the next guy. I love
to see Murderbot find its footing in recognizing that reflection in the
mirror...
